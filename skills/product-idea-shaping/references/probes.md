# Probe bank — product idea shaping

Raw material for the interview. Adapt to the conversation and its language; one question mark per message. Counter-moves override any ordering — probe a vague or unevidenced answer the moment it appears, then return. With `value-proposition.md` and `personas.md` present, the pain, the persona and today's workaround are already answered — do not re-ask them; use them as the material the idea gets tested against.

## Idea on the table

- "Tell me your idea in one sentence — what is it, and what does [persona] do with it first?"
- "What's the very first thing someone sees or clicks?"
- "Who opens it first thing on a Monday — and what for?"
- If the answer is a bundle of features, the feature-list counter-move fires before anything else.

## Pain fit (replay the episode)

- "Take that Tuesday from the personas file — walk me through it again, but now with your product in the room. Where exactly does it step in?"
- "At the moment [persona] hits the pain, what does your product do that the current workaround doesn't?"
- If the walkthrough stalls: "So between [step A] and [step B] — what is your product actually doing there?"
- Past behavior beats hypotheticals: "Tell me about the last time that actually happened."
- "How do they handle this today, step by step?"
- If the mechanism and the pain never meet, name it plainly and kindly, then swing to developing.

## Assumptions (excavate, then rank)

- "For that to work, what has to be true about how [persona] behaves?"
- "Which of these have you actually seen, and which are hopes so far?"
- "Who has to keep this up to date for it to stay useful?"
- Adoption authority — bites hardest for internal tools: "If they wanted this tomorrow, could they just start using it — or who has to say yes?"
- Pre-mortem: "It's six months later and the prototype flopped — tell me the story of why."
- "If you could only test one of these before building, which one scares you most?"
- "What's the cheapest way to find that out before building — a landing page, doing it by hand for a few people, a mock-up?"
- Each excavated assumption gets a status: untested, or tested (with how).

## Differentiation

- "[Persona] already has the workaround — why is switching worth the hassle?"
- "What would have to be true for them to say 'I'm going back to the old way'?"
- "What does the old way still do better?"
- No answer is fine — the section then gets marked open rather than filled with a guess.

## Developing directions (coach proposes, user adopts)

- Derive 2–3 directions from pain + today's workaround, deliberately diverse in mechanism (automate it / make it visible / shortcut the path). Present as your proposals, never as conclusions.
- "None of these might be right — which one is closest, and what's wrong with it?"
- On adoption: "What makes you pick that one?" The stated reason is what turns a proposal into the user's idea. Adopting a combination of two directions counts as adoption too — the reason still has to be stated.
- Adopted directions go back through the pain-fit and assumption probes above.
- Discarded directions go to the *Discarded directions* section with one line of why.

## Soft scope cut (one question, near the end)

- "What's the smallest version that still relieves the top pain?"
- Follow-up only if the answer is still a full product: "What could you leave out and still learn the same thing?"

## Counter-moves

Adapted from the value-proposition interview and persona refinement:

| Answer pattern | Counter-move |
|---|---|
| Group label ("the managers", "companies like Bechtle") | "Who specifically — name one person or one concrete role." |
| Narrowing deflected | Pivot to episode: "Who did you meet most recently from that group?" |
| Abstraction ("they're busy", "it's inefficient") | "Give me the last real example of that." |
| Hypothetical trait ("they probably use Excel") | "Have you seen that — or is it a guess?" (Guesses are fine — they get "(assumed)".) |
| Secondhand story | Ground once: "What did she say herself?" Proxy-only → Open assumptions, "unconfirmed with X". |
| Appeal to prior validation | "What did that look like — what did you actually see or hear?" |
| Jargon or insider term | Get the concrete example behind it before moving on. |
| Proposer benefits from their own product | Ask once what the problem costs *them* personally. |

New for this skill:

| Answer pattern | Counter-move |
|---|---|
| Feature-list answer | "If only one of those can stay — which one?" |
| "They'd surely love it" | "How do you know — who showed or told you that?" |
| Idea drift mid-conversation | "That's a different idea than five minutes ago — deliberate pivot, or shall we finish judging the first one?" |
| "No permission needed / nobody has to approve it" | "For just you, sure — but for it to reach everyone else: who owns the place it would live, and who has to say yes?" |

## Tone examples

Good (acknowledges specifically, replays the episode from the file, one question, no framework speak):

> "Right — so the dashboard would already be open when the ticket lands. Back to that Tuesday from your personas file: Jonas gets the escalation call at 4pm and starts digging through three tools. Where exactly does your dashboard step into that afternoon?"

Bad (bundled questions, framework speak, re-asking what the files already answer):

> "Let's check the pain-fit axis. What's Jonas's top pain? And who is your target group again? Then I'll rate the differentiation."
