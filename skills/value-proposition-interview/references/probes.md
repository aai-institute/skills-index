# Probe bank

Raw material for interview questions. Adapt wording to the conversation and its language — never read these out mechanically or in fixed order. The interview has a natural arc: user → job → pain → today's workaround → gain → (only then) solution fit. **Counter-moves override the arc**: probe a vague pattern the moment it appears, then return to where you were. Every probe below is one question — keep it that way when adapting (one question mark per message).

## Opening

- "Tell me about [the idea] — what got you started on it?"
- If the idea was already described in the invocation: pick the most concrete detail and dig in there instead of asking for a general pitch.

## Finding the primary user

- "Who would be the very first person to use this — not the eventual audience, the first one?"
- "Picture the person who needs this most: what does their Tuesday look like?"
- "If you could only build this for one person in the building, who?"
- "Who complains about this problem today — unprompted?"
- If a narrowing question gets deflected, switch from definition-shaped to episode-shaped: "Who complained most recently — and can you tell me that story?"

## The job

- "What is [user] actually trying to get done when they'd reach for this?"
- "What just happened, right before they need this?"
- "What does 'done' look like for them?"
- Watch for social/emotional jobs behind functional ones: "What does getting this right do for them with their boss or their team?"

## Pains

- "What's the hardest part of that today?"
- "When did that last go wrong — walk me through it."
- "What does that cost them — time, money, nerves?"
- "What do they complain about when this comes up over coffee?"
- If the user benefits from their own product: "And what does it cost *you* personally when that happens?"
- Rank silently: which pain is *extreme* vs. merely annoying? Probe the extreme one.

## Today's alternative

- "How do they handle this right now, without your product?"
- "Why doesn't today's way cut it — or does it mostly work?" (If it mostly works, that's a red flag — say so kindly and probe what would really drive switching.)
- "What have they already tried and then abandoned — and why?"

## The switching gain

- "What single improvement would make them actually change their habits?"
- "If it works, what's observably different a month in?"
- "How do they measure success in this area today?"

## Counter-moves for vague answers

| Answer pattern | Counter-move |
|---|---|
| Group label ("researchers", "all PMs", "everyone") | "Who specifically — name one person or one concrete role." |
| Narrowing question deflected ("I don't want to exclude anyone") | Don't re-ask a definition-shaped variant; pivot to an episode: "Who complained about this most recently — unprompted?" |
| Abstraction ("inefficient", "no transparency") | "Give me the last real example of that." |
| Hypothetical user need ("they would probably want…") | "Has anyone actually said that — and what were their words?" |
| Secondhand story ("Ben spent a whole day on…") | Ground it once: "What did Ben say about it himself?" Proxy-only stories go to Open assumptions as "unconfirmed with Ben". |
| Appeal to prior validation ("we validated this internally") | "What did that validation look like — what did you actually see or hear?" |
| Feature dump | "Park the features for a second — what problem made you think of them?" |
| Unverifiable benefit ("saves time", "better quality") | "How much, compared to what — and how would we see it?" |
| "I'm not sure" | Reassure, then approach from a different angle (an anecdote, a comparison) instead of repeating the question. |
| Answer that contradicts an earlier one | Surface it gently: "Earlier you said X, now Y — help me put those together." |

## Tone examples

Good acknowledgment + question:

> "So the grant reports are the moment it hurts most — that's useful. When you wrote the last one, what part took the longest?"

Bad (leading, bundled, framework-speak):

> "Great! So the main pain is reporting, right? And what are the customer's gains? Also, who else is affected?"
