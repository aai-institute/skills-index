# Probe bank — persona refinement

Raw material for the gap interview. Adapt to the conversation and its language; one question mark per message. Counter-moves override any ordering — probe vagueness the moment it appears. With a VP file, most jobs/pains questions are already answered — do not re-ask them; dig for the persona-level texture below.

## Real people (the anchor)

- "Who from that group have you actually met — tell me about the last one."
- "When you picture the persona, whose face do you see?"
- "How many people like this have you talked to, roughly — one, a handful, dozens?" (**standalone-only** — with a VP file this is usually recorded there, often under Open assumptions; check before asking.)
- If none: say so kindly and continue — the output will be a proto-persona and honest about it.

## Context

- "Walk me through a normal Tuesday for that person."
- "At what moment in their day would your product actually show up?"
- "Who do they answer to, and who comes to them for help?"
- "What's on their desk or screen most of the day?"

## Today's behavior

- "When this problem hits them, what do they literally do first?"
- "Where do they go for answers — colleagues, Google, a vendor, nobody?"
- "What tools or routines have they built around the problem?"

## Decision power & trigger

- "If they wanted your product tomorrow, could they just decide that — or who says yes?"
- "What has to happen for this to jump to the top of their list?"
- "When they last adopted something new, what tipped them?"
- "What would make them hesitate even if they love it?"

## Not our user

- "Is there a group people assume this is for — but it really isn't?"
- Anything ruled out here goes to the "Not our user" section, never to Secondary personas.

## Distinguishing secondary personas

Only explore when the user keeps describing two genuinely different people:

- "Are those two the same person, or two different people you're describing?"
- "If you could only build for one of them first, which one?"
- A different job title alone is not a distinct persona — different jobs, pains, or decision paths are.
- A beneficiary who never uses the product (often the proposer) is NOT a secondary persona — they go to the Beneficiaries line.

## Counter-moves (unchanged from the value-proposition interview)

| Answer pattern | Counter-move |
|---|---|
| Group label ("the managers", "companies like Bechtle") | "Who specifically — name one person or one concrete role." |
| Narrowing deflected | Pivot to episode: "Who did you meet most recently from that group?" |
| Abstraction ("they're busy", "not tech-savvy") | "Give me the last real example of that." |
| Hypothetical trait ("they probably use Excel") | "Have you seen that — or is it a guess?" (Guesses are fine — they get "(assumed)".) |
| Secondhand story | Ground once: "What did she say herself?" Proxy-only → Open assumptions, "unconfirmed with X". |
| Appeal to prior validation | "What did that look like — what did you actually see or hear?" |
| Invented-feeling color ("he's a family guy, drives a Passat") | Ask where it comes from; if it doesn't affect product decisions, leave it out entirely. |

## Tone examples

Good:

> "So the Product Owner just wandered in without an agenda — that matches the value proposition notes. When he stopped at something, what was he actually looking at?"

Bad (re-asking the VP file, bundled, framework-speak):

> "What are his main pains? And what's his job to be done? I'm filling in the persona canvas."
